OpenDrop 1.0 is the first stable release. Earlier builds were previews. This post covers what 1.0 does, what changed since the previews, and what is not finished yet. The changelog page has the same changes as a plain list.

The headline: encryption everywhere, no exceptions

File contents are encrypted on the sending device before they leave it. The cipher is ChaCha20-Poly1305, the IETF AEAD construction from RFC 8439. The key is derived with HKDF-SHA256 from the secret your devices share when you pair them, and each transfer gets a random nonce prefix so no nonce repeats inside it.

Who holds that secret depends on the plan. On Free, it is made on your computer and shared only through the QR code. OpenDrop's servers never receive it. On Pro, OpenDrop's account service also stores it, so a new device you sign in to can join your others.

Encryption is mandatory on every route:

  • Phone to computer on the same Wi-Fi
  • Peer-to-peer links between different networks
  • Relay transfers, chunked or streamed, uploads and downloads
  • Thumbnails loaded from another device
  • Phone to phone over nearby Wi-Fi or Bluetooth Low Energy, and computer to computer (Pro)

On iPhone and iPad the cipher runs through Apple CryptoKit. On Android 9 (API 28) and later it uses BoringSSL, and older Android versions use a pure Dart fallback. Every implementation writes the same wire format, so any of your devices can decrypt what another one sent.

Relay servers carry file contents only in encrypted form. They can see transfer details such as file names, folder names, file sizes and device names, which they need to route transfers.

Every 1.0 app and server speaks protocol version 2, and outdated apps are asked to update. There is no plaintext fallback and no setting that turns encryption off.

Auto-fallback transport: Wi-Fi, peer-to-peer, and the cloud

You never pick a route. OpenDrop tries the routes below and uses the first one that works. The order depends on your plan, as explained after the list.

  • Same Wi-Fi. After you pair with a QR code, your phone finds your computer through mDNS (the _opendrop._tcp.local. service) and streams files directly at full speed. No chunking and no size cap, on either plan.
  • Peer-to-peer. On different networks, OpenDrop tries a direct WebRTC link. NAT traversal uses OpenDrop's own STUN servers. There is no TURN server, so when a network blocks direct links the transfer goes over the relay.
  • Encrypted relay. Both apps dial out to OpenDrop's relay. There is no port forwarding, no tunnel program to install and no public URL to share.
  • Bluetooth Low Energy (Pro). With no Wi-Fi or internet, two phones signed in to the same Pro account can send files over Bluetooth, iPhone or Android. Computers always connect over Wi-Fi or the internet.

Free goes same Wi-Fi, then peer-to-peer, then the shared free relay. Away from your Wi-Fi, Free transfers are speed-limited and move in resumable 5 MB pieces, and transfers over the free relay share a weekly allowance. Pro goes same Wi-Fi, then the faster Pro relay (with backup relays behind it), then peer-to-peer, then Bluetooth between two phones. Pro streams whole files with no weekly limit. Neither plan has a file size cap, and on both a dropped connection picks up where it left off.

Cross-platform parity at launch

1.0 ships as the same version on iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows and Linux. The mobile apps are built with Flutter. The desktop app is the same Python and Qt (PySide6) program on Windows and Linux, and it bundles ffmpeg for media conversion. macOS is not released yet.

What OpenDrop Pro adds

Free needs no account. Each phone pairs with one computer by scanning its QR code, transfers on the same Wi-Fi run at full speed, and transfers across networks are speed-limited. OpenDrop Pro is $2.99 a month or $24.99 a year in the Windows and Linux app. The App Store and Google Play show your price before you subscribe. Pro adds:

  • Multi-device mesh. Every phone, tablet and computer signed in to your account connects automatically, with no QR code. That includes phone to phone (iPhone and Android) and computer to computer.
  • Faster remote transfers. The Pro relay streams whole files at full speed with no weekly limit, and has backup relays.
  • Bluetooth between phones when there is no Wi-Fi or internet.
  • Folders. Browse your computer's subfolders with breadcrumbs, create folders, send or download whole folders from your phone, and open shared folders on your computer from your phone or your other computers.
  • Media conversion. Images (HEIC, HEIF, JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, TIFF), video (MOV, MP4, M4V, AVI, MKV, WMV, FLV, WebM, or MP3 audio out) and RAW camera files (DNG, CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF and more). Conversion always runs on a computer. Queue phone files and they convert as soon as your computer is reachable, then come back to the phone. Conversion history with undo puts the original back on the computer that converted it.
  • One subscription for every device. Pro is tied to your OpenDrop account, so it works on every device you sign in to, whether you subscribed through the App Store, Google Play or the desktop app. Sign in with Google, Apple, Microsoft or email.

UX and theming

Nine color themes ship with 1.0 on both plans. OpenDrop (the default), Ocean Breeze, Tide Pool, Sea Glass and Lilac Night are dark. Cloud Blue, Peach Cream, Rose Petal and Lavender Mist are light. The desktop app also has a Custom theme with a color picker. You pick the theme yourself; it does not follow the system's light or dark setting.

Other additions, on both plans unless marked:

  • Thumbnails for images, RAW photos, videos, PDFs and SVGs, in the desktop app and when you browse your computer from your phone. Folder preview tiles in the desktop app are Pro.
  • A built-in viewer in the mobile app for photos, PDFs and text or code files. Other files open in the app of your choice.
  • Multi-select on mobile to send, download, share or delete several files at once.
  • Recently Deleted: restore files you deleted on your phone or computer.
  • Drag files onto a device tab in the desktop app to send them. Dragging between the Files and Shared Folders tabs, and into subfolders, is Pro.
  • Star favorite devices in the desktop app so they show first. Devices with the same name get a short ID tag.

New privacy controls

  • Analytics opt-out. The app has no ads. It sends basic usage statistics (active users, app version and platform, error counts) to OpenDrop's own server. Turn it off in Settings with Disable basic analytics.
  • No account on Free. You only sign in to subscribe to Pro. You can delete your account at any time from Settings in the app or on the account deletion page.
  • Cookie banner on nfdgames.com using Google Consent Mode v2. Analytics stay off until you accept, and the browser's Global Privacy Control signal is honored.
  • Vulnerability Disclosure Policy with coordinated disclosure timelines and safe-harbor terms for security researchers.
  • EU/UK 14-day withdrawal form using the model form from Directive 2011/83/EU, Annex I(B). See the withdrawal page.
  • Public status page, refreshed every 15 minutes by an automatic check of each service OpenDrop depends on.

Headless and CLI mode

Use the desktop app, or run it headless with opendrop serve (add --background to detach). CLI commands find whichever OpenDrop is running on the same computer through a private local connection file. Commands that reach another device (-d) need Pro. The CLI comes with every desktop build, so there is nothing extra to install.

opendrop serve --background
opendrop status
opendrop ls
opendrop upload report.pdf

The CLI guide covers scripting and exit codes.

If you have an older build

Builds before 1.0 cannot talk to 1.0. A computer running 1.0 answers an older client with HTTP 426 Upgrade Required. There is no compatibility mode, because one would mean keeping an unencrypted path. Install the current build from the download page.

From 1.0 on, the desktop downloads check for updates at launch and install a new version in one click, verified with SHA-256. The Microsoft Store version tells you when an update is out and opens the Store page.

What's still in flight

  • macOS is not released. It is planned for the Mac App Store, for Apple Silicon Macs on macOS 12 or later. Until then, iPhone and iPad work with a Windows or Linux computer.
  • Background transfers are partial. Remote uploads from your phone keep going in the background on iPhone, and for a while on Android. Same-Wi-Fi and peer-to-peer transfers, and all downloads to the phone, need the app open.
  • Discovery is local-network-aware, not location-aware. Devices find each other through QR pairing and mDNS on Wi-Fi, through your account on Pro, or over Bluetooth between Pro phones. There is no location-based discovery.
