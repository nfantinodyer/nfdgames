  2. Information We Collect

    We collect only the information needed to operate the Service. The categories below match the disclosures in our Apple App Privacy nutrition labels and Google Play Data safety section.

    A. Account & Authentication Information.

    • Email address, provided by your chosen sign-in provider (Google, Apple, Microsoft, or direct email/password via Firebase Authentication). Used to identify your account and to link your mobile and desktop devices to the same user.
    • Authentication-provider user ID (for example, your Firebase UID or Google user ID). Used to verify sign-in and to enforce access control between your own devices. We receive this from your authentication provider; we do not receive your password.
    • Truncated, salted hash of your account identifier (a 16-character SHA-256-derived "account hash"). Broadcast on your local network so that the OpenDrop apps on your own devices can recognize one another for LAN discovery. The hash cannot be reversed to recover your email or user ID by any practical means.

    B. Device & Connection Information.

    • Device identifier, a randomly generated, per-installation identifier (the "device_id") used to distinguish each device you own from your other devices. It is not an Apple IDFA / IDFV and is not used for advertising.
    • Device name, the human-readable name you give a device (defaulting to the operating-system name, e.g. "Nicholas's iPhone"). Used so you can pick the right destination when transferring files.
    • Platform & app version, for example, "iOS 18.4 / OpenDrop 1.0.0".
    • Relay address, the connection endpoint your devices use to reach each other through the OpenDrop relay when they are on different networks, stored so your mobile device can find your desktop.
    • Local network address & port (LAN IP / port), stored so that when two of your devices are on the same network, the mobile app can connect directly without going through the cloud.
    • HMAC shared secret, a randomly generated secret used to sign requests between your devices and to derive the end-to-end encryption keys used for file contents. We store this in our key-value store so each of your devices can prove its identity to the others. The secret authenticates only your devices to one another; we cannot decrypt your files without it.
    • Local network broadcast (mDNS). Within your local network, devices running OpenDrop announce their presence over multicast DNS (_opendrop._tcp.local.), including the device name and account hash. This information is broadcast only on your local network and does not leave that network.

    C. Subscription & Purchase Information.

    • Purchase platform identifier and entitlement state (free vs. OpenDrop Pro, subscription expiry date). Provided through Apple App Store In-App Purchase, Google Play Billing, Stripe Payment Links (desktop), and RevenueCat. We do not receive or store your payment card number, bank account, or full billing address. We receive only a confirmation of whether your subscription is active and when it expires.

    D. File Contents (End-to-End Encrypted in Transit).

    • When you transfer a file between your own devices, the contents are end-to-end encrypted using ChaCha20-Poly1305 IETF AEAD (RFC 8439) with a 256-bit content key derived from your account's shared secret via HKDF-SHA256. A fresh random nonce prefix is generated for each transfer. The encryption happens on the sending device before the bytes leave it, and only the receiving device (which holds the same shared secret) can decrypt them. We never see the plaintext of your file contents. The encrypted bytes traverse one of several channels depending on network conditions: (i) direct LAN / Wi-Fi over TLS, (ii) a direct peer-to-peer (WebRTC) connection across networks, established using our own STUN servers for NAT traversal, (iii) our own WebSocket relay over TLS when a direct connection isn't possible (the Fly.io relay for Pro subscribers, the Oracle Cloud relay for the free tier and Pro failover), or (iv) Bluetooth Low Energy when no shared network is available. Encryption is applied on every one of these channels. There is no transfer path that moves plaintext bytes. Relay nodes hold encrypted bytes only transiently while routing them and do not persist file contents. The v1.0 client and server refuse to send or accept unencrypted file bodies; pre-1.0 clients must update before they can transfer files.

  5. Data Security

    • End-to-end encryption of file contents. File bytes are encrypted on the sending device using ChaCha20-Poly1305 IETF AEAD (RFC 8439, an authenticated cipher) with a 256-bit content key derived from your account's shared secret via HKDF-SHA256. A fresh random nonce prefix is generated per transfer. Encryption is applied on every file-content transfer path: chunked uploads, streaming uploads, downloads (including range-sliced chunks), thumbnails, device-to-device relays, local Wi-Fi / LAN, and Bluetooth Low Energy. The encryption runs on Apple CryptoKit (iOS / macOS) or Android's BoringSSL (API 28+) where available, with a pure-Dart fallback elsewhere; the wire format is identical across implementations. Only the receiving device (which holds the same shared secret) can decrypt the bytes. Our infrastructure routes ciphertext only and cannot read your files.
    • No plaintext fallback. The v1.0 app release (mobile and desktop) advertises and enforces protocol version 2 of the OpenDrop wire format, which makes end-to-end encryption mandatory on every file-content path. The v1.0 client and server refuse to send or receive unencrypted file bodies. Pre-1.0 clients running protocol version 0 or 1 (which predate end-to-end encryption) are rejected with an "Upgrade Required" response and must update to a v1.0+ build before they can transfer files. This removes the downgrade-attack surface where an attacker could otherwise strip the encryption header and force an unencrypted body.
    • Filename privacy is out of scope. End-to-end encryption protects file contents. File names, paths, sizes, and modification timestamps remain visible to the metadata layer (Cloudflare worker, KV records, the connecting relay) so the service can route requests and present folder listings. If filename privacy is a requirement for your use case, encrypt the filename into the file contents yourself before transferring.
    • Transport-layer encryption. Beyond end-to-end encryption of file contents, all connections between your devices, our edge services, and our infrastructure use TLS 1.2 or TLS 1.3, including the WebSocket relay connections your devices open to us.
    • Authenticated requests. Application-layer requests between your devices and our infrastructure are signed using HMAC-SHA256 with your per-account shared secret, so that only your devices can act on your account.
    • Authentication. Sign-in is performed by Firebase Authentication using industry-standard OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect flows. Passwords (where you choose email/password sign-in) are handled by Firebase and never visible to us.
    • Storage of secrets. Tokens and the per-account HMAC secret are stored on your device in OS-provided secure storage where available (iOS Keychain, Android Keystore, flutter_secure_storage) or in a permission-restricted local file otherwise.
    • Limits of security. No system can guarantee absolute security. You are responsible for keeping your sign-in credentials and shared secret confidential and for securing your own devices (screen lock, OS updates, etc.).

  7. Data Retention

    We retain personal information only for as long as needed for the purpose for which it was collected, or as required by law.

    • Account record. Retained while your account is active. Deleted within 30 days of an account-deletion request (see Section 11), except where we must keep limited records for tax, accounting, fraud prevention, or legal-defense purposes (typically up to seven years for purchase records).
    • Device records in the key-value store. A device record is automatically pruned 30 minutes after it last reported a heartbeat (the "stale device" cutoff). When you sign out or remove a device, its record is removed immediately.
    • Relay addresses, LAN address, and HMAC secrets. Stored only as part of the active device record; removed when the device record is pruned or you sign out.
    • File contents. Not retained. Encrypted file chunks pass through the relay only for the duration of a transfer (typically seconds to minutes) and are discarded immediately afterward. Files at rest live only on your own devices.
    • Anonymous heartbeats. Individual heartbeat pings are retained for 15 minutes. Daily aggregate counts are retained for 48 hours, weekly for 8 days, and monthly for 35 days. All analytics data is subject to automatic expiration.
    • Support correspondence. Retained for up to 24 months after the conversation closes, unless you ask us to delete it sooner.
    • Edge logs (Cloudflare, Fly.io, Oracle Cloud). Connection and request logs are managed by those providers under their own retention schedules. We do not import or retain their raw access logs.
